# StillStack user requirements

What the households, caretakers and builders of SSK-PRB-001 need from a solar still, written from their side. The product requirements that meet these needs are in SSK-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`), and the links are in the generated traceability matrix SSK-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). Method: STANDARDS section 22. Priorities are proposals for review with the first co-design partner.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Enough water from one panel | One panel gives a household of two its drinking and cooking water, about 10 L on a clear day | High | Household (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Better than a basin still | The panel makes clearly more water per square metre than the basin still people already know, by reusing the heat it collects | High | Household; NGO (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-003 | Safety | Low-salt distillate | The water that comes out has almost no salt, whatever the feed | High | Household; clinic (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-004 | Safety | Brine never reaches drinking water | Salty water cannot get into the distillate outlet, and the two outlets cannot be mixed up | High | Clinic caretaker (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-005 | Deployment and assembly | No power or fuel | Runs on sunlight alone, filled by hand from a bucket or container | High | Household; outpost (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-006 | Environment and durability | Real feed water | Accepts seawater or brackish well water after a cloth filter, without salt clogging it | High | Island outpost; household (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-007 | Safety | Safe materials | Nothing that touches the distillate adds anything harmful to it | High | Household; health authority (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-008 | Environment and durability | Survives a dry day | Forgetting to fill the feed on a sunny day does not ruin the panel | High | Household; caretaker (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-009 | Logistics | Two people can move it | Two people can carry the panel, and it fits a small yard or roof | Medium | Household; outpost (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-010 | Maintenance | Easy cleaning | One person rinses and refits the wicks without special tools in under half an hour, at most once a week | Medium | Clinic caretaker (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-011 | Usability | Works at my latitude | The panel can be tilted to suit the site, from the tropics to about 35 degrees | Medium | Household; NGO (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-012 | Cost | Affordable and buildable locally | A community workshop can build it from local materials with hand tools and a 3D printer at a cost an NGO can fund | High | NGO, makerspace or college (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-013 | Safety | Stays put and does not burn | The panel stays on its stand in a strong breeze, and the hot parts are guarded by instructions and a cover | High | Household; caretaker (PRB Table 2) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
